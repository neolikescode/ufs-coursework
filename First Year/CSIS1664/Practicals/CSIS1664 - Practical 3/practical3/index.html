Welcome to CampusBite. This is where students share simple meal ideas that work in a residence kitchen, in a shared flat, or in a room with nothing more than a kettle.

Everything here is built around three ideas: cook with what you have, plan before you shop, and keep it quick enough to fit around lectures.

Kitchen Basics

A small kitchen works best when it is kept simple. Store dry goods in sealed containers, wash up as you go, and keep one sharp knife rather than a drawer full of blunt ones.

Shared kitchens need a shared routine. Agree on who cleans what, label anything in the fridge that belongs to you, and never leave a pot soaking overnight.

Keep a bottle of H2O in the fridge so that cold water is always within reach, and pour a glass before you start cooking.1

Plan Ahead

The simplest habit to build is what students here call cook once, eat twice, which means making enough of an evening meal to carry into the next day as lunch.

Write your list before you go shopping, and then shop from the list rather than from the shelves.

A first-year student described the change like this:

I used to buy something to eat every single day and still end up with an empty cupboard by Thursday. Now I plan on a Sunday, I shop once, and I am never caught out.
