Orbit Observatory

Public stargazing under the Free State night sky

About the observatory

The Orbit Observatory opened its dome to the public in 2019 and has since welcomed more than forty thousand visitors. Our 0.5 metre reflecting telescope sits well outside the city lights, which means that on a clear winter evening you can see the rings of Saturn, the cloud belts of Jupiter, and the craters along the terminator of the Moon with your own eyes.

Every viewing session is led by one of our resident astronomers. You do not need any equipment, any prior knowledge, or any experience of astronomy. You need only a warm jacket and a little patience whilst your eyes adjust to the dark.

Visit us

The observatory lies eighteen kilometres north-east of Bloemfontein on the Dewetsdorp road. The final four kilometres are gravel, so please allow a little extra time and drive with your headlights dipped once you pass the gate.

Sessions run from March to October, when the nights are long enough and the sky is at its clearest. Entry is free for schools and for registered students.
